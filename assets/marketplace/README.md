# Marketplace assets

Google Marketplace screenshots must show real, verified functionality. Capture them after Phase 1
and Phase 3 using synthetic journalism content—never customer documents.

Shot 03 is the one that carries the multilingual claim: an Arabic document beside an English sidebar
shows that editorial output follows the project's language while the interface stays wherever the
journalist set it. Shot 05 is the weakest — the document is nearly empty because the wizard runs
before anything is written — so it belongs last in the listing order if it is used at all.

The source capture was 1902×890. It was cropped to 1328×830 — exactly 1.600, then resampled down to
1280×800 — rather than stretched, because resampling a 2.14 aspect into 1.60 visibly softens text and
a reviewer will see it. The crop was chosen from measured boundaries, not by eye: the top edge sits in
a clean band below the menu row so no menu label is cut in half, the right edge clears the sidebar
panel at x=1806 while excluding the account avatar, and the left edge sits 4px before the leftmost
document ink.

The sources for 02-05 are roughly 2.2 aspect, wider than shot 01, so they are scaled to fit and
centred on white rather than cropped to exactly 1.60. Cropping that hard would have cut either the
document text or the sidebar; the white bars cost about 10% of the height and lose nothing.

The account's credit balance was cleared from the Access card in every shot. The card background is
pure white, so nothing about the interface is misrepresented — the card still shows its real state.
This is required by the billing-details rule below. Sample the background from *inside* the card when
redoing this: the sidebar behind the card is beige, and painting beige over white leaves a bar that
reads as censorship.

Two sidebar-only captures were taken on 2026-08-11 and not used. At 435x795 and 456x761 they are
portrait, and fitting them to a 1280x800 landscape frame would leave the interface stranded in the
middle of two thirds white. A sidebar shot needs the document beside it to mean anything anyway.

Do not add:

- copied Google product logos or protected third-party marks;
- customer names, emails, licence codes, project IDs, or billing details;
- material from private investigations;
- mock screenshots that imply unfinished functions already work.
